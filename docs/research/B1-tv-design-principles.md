# B1 – Core design principles in four TV design guides
 
Read 2026-11-20, makers' own pages only. All four guides were reachable. Every row is [C] (primary page read).
 
| Source | Principle (their wording, short) | What it means in practice | Link |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google | "Content front and center" | Content first, least friction to reach it | [C] https://developer.android.com/design/ui/tv/guides/foundations/design-for-tv |
| Google | "10ft UI" | Text large enough from about 3 m, or little text | same |
| Google | "D-pad navigation" | Up/down/left/right/select only; instant, distinct feedback on every press | same |
| Google | "TV is a communal device" | Shared screen; privacy settings for personal info | same |
| Google | "Efficient" | Fewest clicks and screens to content | [C] https://developer.android.com/training/tv/get-started/navigation |
| Google | "Predictable" | Don't reinvent navigation patterns | same |

## Notes
- Only Samsung (Application Design Principles) and LG (Design Principles) label theirs "principles." Google's are headed "Considerations" (plus a separate navigation "Principles" list). Apple's are "Best practices."
- Apple also lists five "device characteristics" (display, ergonomics, inputs, app interactions, system features); omitted as descriptions, not principles.
- Apple's page was read as Markdown (the web page needs JavaScript). Google, Samsung and LG pages came through a summarizing fetch tool, so short quotes should be spot-checked.
- LG's page shows as about 10 years old in search metadata; may predate current webOS.
